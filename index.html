About Me

Who I Am

I am a PhD researcher at the University of Geneva, working under the supervision of Arnaud Casteigts in the area of algorithmic graph theory. My research focuses on temporal graphs — dynamic structures where edges are present only at certain times — with a particular interest in reachability, connectivity, and the complexity of problems in this setting.

I hold an ESKAS (Swiss Government Excellence) scholarship, which funds my doctoral research in Switzerland. Before my PhD, I worked on problems at the intersection of algorithms and fairness, particularly in the context of rank aggregation under fairness constraints in computational social choice.

My broader intellectual curiosity extends to cryptography, where I am interested in the interplay between combinatorial structures and computational security.

Research

What I Work On

⟳

Temporal Graphs

Studying graphs where edges appear and disappear over time. Key questions involve reachability, temporal paths, spanners, and the complexity of optimization problems in time-varying networks.

⚖

Computational Social Choice

Algorithmic and axiomatic aspects of collective decision-making. Previously worked on fair rank aggregation — finding consensus rankings that satisfy fairness constraints across groups.

🔐

Cryptography

Broadly interested in the foundations of cryptography, particularly in how combinatorial and graph-theoretic structures can inform the design and analysis of secure protocols.
